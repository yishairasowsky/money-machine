# Track B — Content engine

A repeatable process for turning one niche into a steady stream of publishable drafts, plus ten real, ready-to-edit drafts to prove the process works. Nothing here posts anywhere automatically — you review and publish each piece yourself, on accounts this repo doesn't have access to.

**Niche chosen for this prototype:** personal finance for beginners (evergreen search demand, realistic affiliate/ad monetization paths, low topical risk). Swap the niche in `PROCESS.md` and re-run the process for anything else.

## What's here

- `PROCESS.md` — the repeatable method: how a topic becomes a calendar slot becomes a draft.
- `content_calendar.csv` — a posting schedule (2026-09-01 through 2026-10-02) with all 10 drafts below slotted in, roughly twice a week.
- `drafts/` — 10 real drafts, ready for you to fact-check, add your own voice/examples, and publish.

## Honest economics

Content income is not passive on day one — it's deferred effort: you front-load writing/editing now, and (if the audience shows up) it pays off with less marginal work later via affiliate links, ads, or your own product. Realistic timelines to meaningful income are usually months, not days, and most attempts don't reach meaningful traffic at all. Treat this as a real but slow-and-uncertain track, not the fast one.

## Fact-check pass (2026-09-04)

The README has said "fact-check each draft before publishing" since these were written — that step had never actually been done. Went through all 10 drafts and checked every specific number against the formula or source each draft cites (mortgage amortization, contribution/growth math, credit-score tiers, FICO weighting). One real error found: **draft 03 (compound interest)** stated its two headline numbers ($720k/$340k) using annual-lump-sum compounding math while describing monthly $300 contributions — the correct monthly-compounding result is **$787k/$366k**, about 8-9% higher. Fixed in the draft itself, with a note on what changed. The other 9 drafts' worked numbers (mortgage payments, loan-payoff/investment comparisons, HSA growth) all check out against their own stated formulas and assumptions; their existing "verify before publishing" notes on year-specific figures (contribution limits, current rates) still stand — those genuinely can't be verified without a live data source, which this sandbox doesn't have.
