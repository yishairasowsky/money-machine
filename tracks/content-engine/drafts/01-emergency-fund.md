# How much should a beginner keep in an emergency fund?

You've heard "3-6 months of expenses" so many times it's stopped meaning anything. Here's how to actually pick a number.

**Start with $1,000, not 6 months.** If you have any debt above ~7% interest, a starter fund of $1,000 is usually enough to stop a small emergency (car repair, vet bill) from becoming new credit card debt. Build that first, fast, before anything else.

**Then size the real fund to your risk, not a slogan:**
- Stable job, dual income, no dependents → 3 months of *essential* expenses (rent, food, insurance, minimum debt payments — not your whole lifestyle) is reasonable.
- Single income, dependents, or commission/freelance income → 6 months, sometimes more.
- Very stable job + strong safety net (family, easy re-employment) → 3 months is often plenty, and more cash sitting idle has a real opportunity cost.

**Calculate essential monthly expenses**, don't guess: pull your last 3 months of bank statements, total rent/mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments, transportation. Multiply by your target months.

**Where to keep it:** a high-yield savings account, not your checking account (too easy to spend) and not the stock market (it can drop right when you need it). You're optimizing for "available tomorrow," not growth.

**Next action:** pull 3 months of statements this week, calculate your real essential-expense number, and set an automatic transfer — even $50/paycheck — into a separate high-yield savings account until you hit your target.
