# How does compound interest actually work (with real numbers)?

"Compound interest is the eighth wonder of the world" doesn't mean much without seeing the actual numbers. Here they are.

**The mechanism:** you earn returns not just on what you put in, but on the returns you already earned. Every year's gain becomes part of next year's base.

**A real comparison, same $300/month, different start dates, both stopping at age 65, assuming a 7% average annual return (a common long-run stock market assumption, not a guarantee):**
- Start at 25: ~40 years of contributions ≈ $144,000 invested → grows to roughly **$720,000**.
- Start at 35: ~30 years of contributions ≈ $108,000 invested → grows to roughly **$340,000**.

Ten years of delay costs roughly half the final total, even though the person who started late only contributed about 25% less. That gap *is* compounding — most of it isn't from contributing more, it's from time.

**The formula, if you want to check it yourself:** future value of a monthly contribution `P` at monthly rate `r` for `n` months ≈ `P × [((1+r)^n − 1) / r]`. For the numbers above, r = 7%/12, n = 480 or 360.

**Why this matters practically:** the single highest-leverage money move for most beginners isn't picking the perfect investment — it's starting now instead of waiting for the "right" moment or a bigger paycheck. Even a small amount started today usually beats a larger amount started in five years.

**Next action:** if you're not yet contributing to any investment account, open one this month (even a small automatic amount) — the account type and amount matter less than the start date.
