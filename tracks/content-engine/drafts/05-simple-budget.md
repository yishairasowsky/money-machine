# A simple beginner budgeting method that isn't a full spreadsheet

Most people quit budgeting because they start with a 40-category spreadsheet. Here's a version that takes 10 minutes a month.

**The 50/30/20 split, as a starting point, not a rule:**
- 50% needs (rent, groceries, utilities, minimum debt payments, insurance)
- 30% wants (everything discretionary — eating out, subscriptions, hobbies)
- 20% savings/extra debt payoff

If your needs are genuinely above 50% (common in high cost-of-living areas), don't force the ratio — protect the savings percentage first and let "wants" shrink instead.

**The 10-minute monthly version:**
1. Look at last month's total spend by category in your bank/card app (most have this built in now — you don't need a new tool).
2. Compare to the 50/30/20 target as rough percentages, not exact dollars.
3. Pick *one* category to adjust next month, not five. One consistent change compounds; five simultaneous changes usually collapse within two weeks.
4. Automate the savings piece — a scheduled transfer the day after payday — so it happens before you can spend it.

**Why this beats a detailed spreadsheet for most beginners:** the value of budgeting is the monthly check-in habit, not precision to the dollar. A method you'll actually repeat every month beats a perfect one you abandon after week two.

**Next action:** open your bank app right now, look at last month's spending by category, and pick the one category you'll adjust this month.
