# What credit score do I actually need?

"Good credit" isn't one number — the score you need depends entirely on what you're trying to do with it.

**The FICO ranges most lenders use:** below 580 is generally considered poor, 580-669 fair, 670-739 good, 740-799 very good, 800-850 exceptional. Most everyday financial products have a real threshold somewhere in the fair-to-good range, not at the top of the scale.

**What each tier actually unlocks:**
- **Mortgages:** conventional loans generally want 620+; FHA loans allow as low as 580 with 3.5% down, and sometimes 500-579 with 10% down. The best conventional rates are usually reserved for 740+.
- **Auto loans:** roughly 660+ is typically treated as "prime" and gets meaningfully better rates; below that, rates climb fast, and the gap between prime and subprime auto APRs can be very large. This gap moves with the broader rate environment, so check current typical rates by tier rather than assuming last year's numbers still hold.
- **Rewards credit cards:** many require 670+; if you're building credit from scratch or recovering from a low score, secured cards are usually available regardless of score and report to the bureaus the same way.
- **Apartment rentals:** many landlords informally look for 620+, though this varies enormously by market and landlord.

**What actually moves your score, in order of weight:** payment history (~35%) matters most — a single 30-day-late payment can be more damaging than almost anything else on this list. Amounts owed relative to your limits, i.e. credit utilization (~30%), is next — keeping reported utilization under 30%, and ideally under 10%, meaningfully helps. Length of credit history (~15%), new credit inquiries (~10%), and credit mix (~10%) round it out.

**The one-line action most people skip:** utilization is calculated from your statement balance, not your current balance, and it's usually reported once a month. Paying a card down before the statement closes — not just before the due date — is what actually lowers what shows up on your report.

**Next action:** pull your free credit report, check your utilization on each card, and if any single card is above 30% of its limit, pay it down before the statement date this month — that's the fastest lever most people can pull.
