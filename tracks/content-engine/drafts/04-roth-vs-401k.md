# Roth IRA vs. 401(k): which should a beginner prioritize?

If your employer offers a 401(k) match, that's the actual first answer — it's an immediate, guaranteed return that beats almost anything else available to you. After that, it gets more interesting.

**The core tradeoff:** a traditional 401(k) is funded pre-tax (lowers your taxable income now, taxed on withdrawal in retirement). A Roth IRA is funded post-tax (no deduction now, but qualified withdrawals in retirement are tax-free — including all the growth).

**A simple decision rule for beginners:**
1. Contribute to your 401(k) up to the full employer match — free money, take it first.
2. If you expect your tax rate to be *higher* in retirement than it is now (common for people early in their career, likely to earn more later), prioritize a Roth IRA next — you're locking in today's lower tax rate on all future growth.
3. If you expect your tax rate to be *lower* in retirement, or you want the tax deduction now, put additional money back into the 401(k) (or a traditional IRA).
4. If you can max both, contribution limits become the real constraint, not this decision — check current-year IRS limits since they change annually.

**Why beginners often skip the 401(k) match by accident:** it usually requires manually enrolling and setting a contribution percentage — it's rarely automatic. If you started a job in the last year and haven't checked, you may be leaving free money on the table right now.

**Next action:** log into your 401(k) provider today and confirm you're contributing at least enough to get the full employer match. If you already are, open a Roth IRA at a low-fee brokerage and automate a monthly contribution.
