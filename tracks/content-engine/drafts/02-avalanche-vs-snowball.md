# Debt avalanche vs. debt snowball: which pays off debt faster?

Both methods have you pay minimums on everything, then throw every extra dollar at one debt at a time. They only differ in *which* debt goes first.

**Avalanche: highest interest rate first.** Mathematically optimal — you pay the least total interest and get out of debt fastest in pure dollar terms. Best if you're motivated by numbers and can stay consistent without quick wins.

**Snowball: smallest balance first**, regardless of interest rate. You pay more interest overall, but you clear your first debt sooner, which is a real psychological win that keeps some people going when avalanche would have them stuck on one stubborn high-rate debt for a year with nothing to show for it.

**A concrete example:** say you owe $500 at 22% APR, $3,000 at 18% APR, and $8,000 at 6% APR.
- Avalanche order: the $3,000 (18%) first, then the $500 (22%)... wait — avalanche always means *highest rate first*, so it's actually $500 (22%) → $3,000 (18%) → $8,000 (6%). This is worth double-checking with your own numbers; it's easy to mis-order by balance instead of rate by accident.
- Snowball order: $500 → $3,000 → $8,000 (smallest balance first) — in this example the two happen to start with the same debt, which won't always be true.

**The real answer:** avalanche saves more money; snowball keeps more people actually finishing. Pick avalanche if you trust yourself to stay motivated without early wins. Pick snowball if past attempts at debt payoff have died from losing motivation, not from the math.
